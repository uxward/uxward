## 1. Summary

This audit found a discrepancy that reframes the entire prior baseline: **the repo and
the live site are two different websites.**

| | Live at `uxward.com` | In this repo |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | Squarespace | Astro 7 → Cloudflare |
| Pages | 119 (sitemap) | 36 |
| `/about` | **404** | exists |

| `/llms.txt` | **404** | present, 4.1 KB |

| JSON-LD blocks | 1 | Person + WebSite on all pages, plus per-page graphs |
| Title | `Brandon E.B. Ward` | `Design practice, built from scratch…` |

**Two scores, because there are two sites:**

- **Live (what AI systems can see today): ~38/100**
- **Repo (what deploys when you cut over): ~72/100**

The June baseline of 40/100 was measured against Squarespace. It was never a baseline for
this repo, and the "improvement to 65–70" recorded in `SEO-TODOs.md` was never live.

**The single highest-value action is deployment.** Roughly 34 points are already built and
sitting undeployed.

## 3. Findings by severity

### Critical

**C1 — The site isn't deployed.** Covered above. Everything below describes the repo,
which is what matters once you cut over.

**C2 — No redirect map exists for the migration.** 119 live URLs; only 11 survive as
direct matches. 13 dated essay paths need 301s, 6 essays have no destination at all, and
80 tag/category archives will 404. There is no `public/_redirects`. Cutting over without
this discards the indexed history of a site dating to 2014. Full map in `SEO-TODOs.md`.

**C3 — Canonical host conflict.** The repo emits apex canonicals
(`https://uxward.com`); the live DNS redirects apex → `www`. If both survive, every
canonical 301s. Decide apex or `www` before cutover, not after.

### High

**H1 — `/colophon` ships two `<h1>` elements.** Lines 81 and 90. The cyber-mode block is
CSS-hidden but present in the DOM, so extractors read "A note on the breach… jacked into
Night City" as page content.

**H2 — Entity data contradicts itself.** 25 years (schema, homepage) vs 20+ years
(`llms.txt`) vs "Many years" (`/about`). AI systems resolving conflicting claims about one
entity tend to hedge or omit. Also: `llms.txt` says 22 essays; there are 23.

**H3 — `/about` has no page-level schema.** The page most likely to answer "who is Brandon
Ward" carries only the global graph. `ProfilePage` + `hasOccupation` + `award` would map
the career timeline already on the page into machine-readable facts.

**H4 — 9.2 MB of unoptimized images.** 101 JPG/PNG files against 16 WebP. Largest single
asset is an 832 KB unsplash JPG. Direct LCP cost.

### Medium

**M1 — `loading="lazy"` on 26/125 images; `width`/`height` on 10/125.** CLS exposure.

**M2 — Work-page meta descriptions run 214–339 chars.** SERP truncation. See the
GEO-vs-SEO trade in `SEO-TODOs.md` §2.2.

**M3 — Heading text concatenates counts.** `"Research & testing4"` and four siblings on
`/work`. Affects both AI extraction and screen readers.

**M4 — `/work` heading hierarchy is semantically inconsistent.** Precocity is `h2`; the
other four case studies are `h3` despite being siblings. This is the visual hero treatment
from `0ca46a9` leaking into document semantics.

**M5 — `Article` schema has no `image`, `dateModified`, or `wordCount`.**

**M6 — `/work` and `/writing` have no `CollectionPage`/`ItemList`.**

**M7 — `/ink` is indexable with the site-default description.** Easter-egg page in the
sitemap with no metadata of its own.

**M8 — Lightbox images never receive an `alt`.** All 5 case pages.

**M9 — No `_headers` file.** No CSP, HSTS, `X-Frame-Options`, or `Referrer-Policy` at the
edge.

### Low

**L1 — One content image with empty `alt`** (`cave.jpeg`).
**L2 — Three essay titles run 79–80 chars.**
**L3 — `datePublished` is quarter-derived**, so every essay resolves to the first day of
its quarter.
**L4 — `publisher` is a `Person`.** Valid, but Google prefers `Organization`.
**L5 — Internal linking is one-directional.** 0 of 23 essays link to `/work`; 1 work page
links to `/writing`. `MoreArticles` relates essays by recency, not topic.

## 4. What's genuinely strong

Worth stating plainly, because the deployment finding shouldn't obscure it — the repo is
in good shape:

- **All JSON-LD is valid** across all 36 pages. Zero parse errors.
- **No `undefined` anywhere in the built HTML.** The `self.deck` concern from the prior
  TODO list is confirmed resolved.
- **120/120 `<img>` tags have `alt` attributes**, and the alt text is descriptive and
  specific ("Four service-design journey maps produced by the Precocity practice"), not
  keyword filler.
- **Content E-E-A-T is the site's real asset.** First-person operator detail, named
  clients, a decade-long arc, and a genuinely rare credential in the FTC v. Match.com
  expert-witness role. This is what most portfolio sites cannot fake and AI systems reward.
- **`llms.txt` is well-constructed** — better than most sites that have one at all.
- **Clean semantic HTML, skip link, `aria-label`s, real heading structure** on most pages.
- **Build is fast and small** — 36 pages in 1.4s, 50 KB largest HTML document.

## 5. Recommended sequence

1. **Decide apex vs `www`** — blocks everything else
2. **Write `public/_redirects`** from the map in `SEO-TODOs.md`
3. **Deploy to Cloudflare, point DNS** — this alone is ~+34 points
4. **Verify** `llms.txt`, `sitemap-index.xml`, `/about`, `/work` return 200
5. **Submit sitemaps** to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools
6. **Fix Tier 1 items** (bugs, entity consistency, schema, images)
7. **Start Tier 3 off-site work** — Wikidata first; it's 15 minutes and it's the ceiling
8. **Re-audit in 30 days** with `/geo compare uxward.com`

Steps 1–3 are worth more than 4–8 combined. Step 7 is the only path above 80.

## 6. Method

- Live site: `curl` for status codes, headers, redirect chains, `robots.txt`, sitemap
- Repo: `npm run build` → static analysis of all 36 built HTML documents
- JSON-LD: parsed every `application/ld+json` block in the build
- Citability: `~/.claude/skills/geo/scripts/citability_scorer.py` against `astro preview`
- URL mapping: live sitemap (119 URLs) diffed against built sitemap (35 URLs)

Live `robots.txt` note, since it's easy to misread: Squarespace stacks ~29 AI user-agents
*above* `User-agent: *` with no intervening `Disallow`. Per spec these form one group
sharing the `*` rules, so **AI crawlers are not blocked** on the live site — the rules are
permissive. This is a non-issue, recorded here to prevent a false alarm.
